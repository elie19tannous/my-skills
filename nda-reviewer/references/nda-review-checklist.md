# NDA Review Checklist

A repeatable pass for reviewing a general (cross-jurisdiction) NDA. The failure
mode this guards against is a document titled "confidentiality agreement" that
quietly assigns IP, grants a broad licence, or binds only one side.

## 0. Context (set before redlining)

- Which side is your client — discloser, recipient, or both?
- Mutual (MNDA) or one-way? Does the actual disclosure flow one way or both?
- Purpose / relationship (evaluation, vendor eval, employment, M&A, partnership).
- What is actually being shared (trade secrets vs routine business info)?
- Deal value & leverage (take-it-or-leave-it changes what's worth fighting).

## 1. Mutuality & balance

Read every obligation from both sides. Flag asymmetry in:
- Confidentiality duty & standard of care.
- Definition of "Confidential Information" (broad for one, narrow for the other).
- Remedies (injunctive relief, indemnity, fee-shifting on one side only).
- Term / survival length per party.
- Termination and return/destroy (binds only one side).

One-way is legitimate only when disclosure genuinely flows one way.

## 2. Definition of Confidential Information

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| "all information disclosed", no limit | Require marking or "reasonable person would treat as confidential" |
| Captures pre-NDA / perpetual info | Bound to the engagement window; add a term |
| No carve-outs | Add the five standard exclusions (§3) |
| Unbounded "derived therefrom" | Tie derivatives to the same purpose + carve-outs |

## 3. The five standard carve-outs (all should be present)

1. Already public (or becomes public with no fault of recipient).
2. Already known to recipient before disclosure (with evidence).
3. Independently developed without use of the confidential information.
4. Rightfully received from a third party with no duty of confidence.
5. Required by law/court order — with notice + cooperation to seek protection.

Missing carve-outs = red flag. #2 and #3 are the most commonly dropped.

## 4. Term, survival & return

- Agreement term ≠ survival of confidentiality obligation (two clocks).
- Survival: 2–5 years for ordinary business info; indefinite ONLY for genuine
  trade secrets, and scoped to trade secrets — not "all Confidential Info forever."
- Flag perpetual obligations over ordinary (non-trade-secret) info as unreasonable.
- Confirm return-or-destroy on termination; one archival copy / backups is common
  and acceptable if still bound by confidentiality.

## 5. IP / licence / feedback traps (highest severity)

| Trap | Pattern | Fix direction |
|------|---------|---------------|
| IP assignment | "recipient hereby assigns…" | Strike; "no IP is assigned" |
| Feedback grab | "feedback becomes discloser's property, perpetual" | "no rights in feedback transferred" |
| Broad licence | "use CI for any purpose" | Limit to the defined purpose only |
| Residuals abuse | one-sided residuals | Make mutual + narrow, or strike |
| Smuggled non-compete/non-solicit | restraint buried in NDA | Negotiate separately; route enforceability to counsel |
| No-improvements grab | "improvements belong to discloser" | Strike; each party keeps its own IP |

Correct default: express statement that no licence or ownership of IP is granted
except the limited right to use the info for the defined purpose.

## 6. Remedies & boilerplate

- Injunctive relief: mutual, not one-sided.
- Indemnity / liquidated damages / fee-shifting: flag one-sidedness; flag
  liquidated damages as a jurisdiction-specific enforceability question (route to counsel).
- Governing law & forum: note but do not opine on enforceability.
- No-assignment of the agreement, notices, entire-agreement — routine cleanup.

## 7. Report

Table sorted by severity: IP/assignment & one-sided remedies → scope/term →
cleanup. Columns: Clause | Issue | Severity | Whose favour | Suggested edit.
Headline: "X high-severity issues; sign / sign-with-edits / do-not-sign."

## 8. Anti-overreach

- A clean mutual NDA is common — say so; don't invent issues.
- Distinguish unfair (negotiate) from unenforceable (legal Q) from unusual-but-OK.
- No jurisdiction-specific enforceability opinions — flag and route to counsel.
- Propose balance, not a mirror-image imbalance favouring the other side.
