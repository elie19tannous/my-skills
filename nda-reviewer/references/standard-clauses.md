# Standard NDA Clauses — Balanced Language & Red Flags

Model language for the common clauses, each paired with the red-flag pattern it
neutralizes. Use as a starting point for the "suggested edit" column; adapt to
the deal and always route jurisdiction-specific questions to counsel.

## Mutual definition of Confidential Information

**Balanced:** "'Confidential Information' means non-public information disclosed
by a party ('Discloser') to the other ('Recipient') that is marked confidential
or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given its nature
and the circumstances of disclosure."

**Red flag:** definition applies to only one party's information; "all
information disclosed, whether or not marked"; captures info disclosed before the
agreement.

## The five carve-outs

**Balanced:** "Confidential Information does not include information that: (a) is
or becomes public through no breach by Recipient; (b) was known to Recipient
before disclosure, as shown by its records; (c) is independently developed by
Recipient without use of the Confidential Information; (d) is rightfully received
from a third party without a duty of confidence; or (e) is required to be
disclosed by law or court order, provided Recipient gives prompt notice and
reasonable cooperation to seek protective treatment."

**Red flag:** no carve-outs; missing (b) or (c); a legal-compulsion carve-out
with no notice/cooperation obligation.

## Permitted use / purpose

**Balanced:** "Recipient may use the Confidential Information solely to [defined
purpose, e.g. evaluate the potential business relationship] and for no other
purpose."

**Red flag:** "for any business purpose"; a licence to use the information beyond
the stated purpose.

## No IP or licence granted (the trap-neutralizer)

**Balanced:** "No licence or other right to any intellectual property is granted
under this Agreement, except the limited right to use the Confidential
Information for the Purpose. All feedback, comments, or suggestions Recipient
provides are given without transfer of any right, title, or interest, and each
party retains ownership of its own intellectual property, including improvements
it independently develops."

**Red flag:** "recipient hereby assigns"; "feedback becomes Discloser's
property"; "improvements to Discloser's IP belong to Discloser."

## Term & survival

**Balanced:** "This Agreement runs for [term]. Recipient's confidentiality
obligations survive for [3–5] years after disclosure; for information that
constitutes a trade secret, the obligations continue for as long as the
information remains a trade secret."

**Red flag:** a single perpetual obligation over all Confidential Information;
conflating agreement term with survival.

## Return or destruction

**Balanced:** "On termination or on Discloser's request, Recipient will return or
destroy the Confidential Information, except that Recipient may retain one
archival copy and routine backups, which remain subject to this Agreement."

**Red flag:** return/destroy obligation binds only one party; no backup/archival
allowance (impractical); no confirmation-of-destruction option.

## Remedies

**Balanced:** "The parties agree that breach may cause irreparable harm for which
damages are inadequate, and either party may seek injunctive relief in addition
to other remedies."

**Red flag:** injunctive relief, indemnity, or fee-shifting available to only one
party; liquidated-damages figure (flag as a jurisdiction-specific enforceability
question — route to counsel).

## Boilerplate to confirm

Governing law & forum (note, do not opine on enforceability); no-assignment of
the agreement; entire-agreement / no oral modification; notices; severability.
Watch for a non-compete or non-solicit smuggled into the boilerplate — pull it
out and negotiate it as a separate, clearly scoped clause.
