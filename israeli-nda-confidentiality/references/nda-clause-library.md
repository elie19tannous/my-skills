# Israeli NDA — Clause Library

Clause-by-clause drafting library for Israeli NDAs. Each entry: purpose, drafting notes, Israeli-specific pitfall.

## 1. Parties (הצדדים)
State full legal names and company registration numbers (ח.פ. for a company, ע.מ./עוסק מורשה for a sole proprietor). Specify whether affiliates, subsidiaries, employees, and outside advisors (accountants, lawyers) are bound as representatives of a party, or need their own confidentiality undertaking.

## 2. Definition of Confidential Information (הגדרת מידע סודי)
Draft broad but bounded: cover written, oral, and visual disclosures; information marked confidential AND information a reasonable recipient should know is confidential even if unmarked. Tie the definition to the "trade secret" (sod mishari) concept under the Commercial Torts Law, 5759-1999 so the strongest statutory remedy (up to 100,000 NIS in statutory damages without proof of actual loss) is available for the information that qualifies. Pitfall: an unbounded "everything, forever" definition is both unfair to the recipient and harder for a court to enforce — it looks less like protecting a real secret and more like a restraint of trade.

## 3. Purpose / Permitted Use (מטרה / שימוש מותר)
State the single defined purpose the information may be used for (e.g. "evaluating a potential partnership," "performing the Services"). This narrows scope and is itself a factor courts weigh when deciding whether a restraint protects a legitimate interest.

## 4. Standard Exclusions (חריגים)
Always include: information already public, information the recipient already lawfully knew before disclosure, information independently developed without reference to the discloser's information, information lawfully received from a third party without a duty of confidentiality, and information the recipient is compelled to disclose by law/regulator/court (with prior notice where lawful). Pitfall: missing exclusions makes an NDA over-broad, harder to enforce, and unfair to the recipient — a lawyer reviewing on the other side will flag this immediately.

## 5. Obligations (חובות)
Keep secret; limit access to personnel with a genuine need-to-know; apply reasonable technical/organizational safeguards; do not copy beyond what the permitted use requires.

## 6. Compelled Disclosure (גילוי על פי דין)
Permit disclosure required by law, regulator, or court order, conditioned on giving the discloser prior notice where lawful so they can seek a protective order or narrow the disclosure.

## 7. Return / Destruction (השבה / השמדה)
On termination of the relationship or on request, the recipient must return or destroy the confidential information and all copies, and confirm this in writing. Consider a carve-out for copies kept solely for legal/compliance archival purposes, clearly still bound by confidentiality.

## 8. Term & Survival (תקופה והישרדות) — two separate clocks
Do not conflate the disclosure period (how long the parties are actively exchanging information) with the confidentiality survival period (how long the duty to keep it secret lasts after the relationship ends). For ordinary business information, a fixed survival of 3–5 years is common. For genuine trade secrets (sod mishari), survival should be indefinite — tied to the information remaining secret and valuable — not capped at an arbitrary short term, since capping releases the secret to the world on an artificial deadline.

## 9. Remedies (סעדים)
Primary remedies: injunctive relief (tzav mnia) to stop ongoing or threatened misuse, plus damages. The Commercial Torts Law, 5759-1999 provides statutory damages up to 100,000 NIS for trade-secret misappropriation without needing to prove actual loss — reference this explicitly for information that qualifies as a trade secret. Agreed (liquidated) damages clauses are enforceable but a court can reduce them under the Contracts (Remedies for Breach) Law, 1970 if they are grossly disproportionate to foreseeable harm — draft them as a genuine pre-estimate, not a penalty.

## 10. No License / No Obligation to Proceed (ללא רישיון / ללא התחייבות)
Disclosure under the NDA grants no license or right in the discloser's IP, and creates no obligation on either party to enter into any further deal, investment, or transaction.

## 11. Non-Compete Caveat (Israel-specific — see the skill's Step 5)
If a non-compete or non-solicit clause is bundled in, keep it narrow, time-limited, and tied to a genuine legitimate interest (real trade secrets, specialized training, or separate consideration paid for the restraint). A bare non-compete whose only purpose is to block ordinary competition is unlikely to be enforced under the Checkpoint/AES Supreme Court line of case law — the confidentiality/trade-secret clause is the load-bearing protection, not the non-compete.

## 12. Governing Law & Jurisdiction (דין וסמכות שיפוט)
Specify Israeli law and a named Israeli court (or arbitration seat) for agreements between Israeli parties. A stray foreign-forum clause copied from a US or generic template creates needless cost, delay, and uncertainty if a dispute arises.
