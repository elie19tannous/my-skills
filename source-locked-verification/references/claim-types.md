# Claim Types — What Needs a Source

Not every sentence needs a citation. Sourcing pure argument is noise; letting a
fact hide inside argument is the real risk. Use this taxonomy to decide.

## Needs a source

| Type | Example | Source it points to |
|------|---------|---------------------|
| Factual | "The shipment arrived on 4 May." | Record evidence, exhibit, business record |
| Quantitative | "Revenue fell 12% in Q3." | Financial statement, data-room file |
| Legal | "Section 7 requires 30 days' notice." | Statute / regulation / case |
| Quotation | "The email said 'we will not proceed.'" | The source document, verbatim |
| Embedded premise | "...because the vendor never delivered." | Record evidence (the non-delivery is a fact) |

## Exempt (do NOT flag as unsourced)

| Type | Example | Why exempt |
|------|---------|------------|
| Argument | "This shows bad faith." | Characterization, not a fact |
| Legal conclusion | "The contract is therefore void." | The court's/author's conclusion |
| Opinion / puffery | "This is the best approach." | Non-falsifiable |
| Self-evident framing | "For the reasons above..." | Structural, no factual content |

## The trap: disguised facts

Argument often carries a hidden factual premise. Treat the premise as a claim
even though the wrapper is argument:
- "Clearly the defendant knew (they had the report on 1 March)" → the
  "had the report on 1 March" is a factual claim needing a source.
- "Given their repeated late payments" → "repeated late payments" is a fact.

## Quotation rule

Quotations are the strictest type: they must match the source verbatim. A
dropped word, an added qualifier, or an ellipsis that changes meaning is a
HIGH-severity defect even when the citation itself is correct.

## Quantity rule

A quantitative claim is not verified by a source that supports only the
surrounding fact. "Signed a contract" being sourced does not verify "for $2.4M."
The amount, date, and count each need their own support.
