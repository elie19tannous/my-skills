# Jurisdiction Map — Rabbinical Court vs. Family Court

## The one rule that matters most
For a Jewish couple, the **Rabbinical Court has exclusive power over the gett** (the divorce itself). Everything else — property, support, custody — can go to **either** the Rabbinical Court or the civil Family Court, and the **first proper filing usually fixes the forum** (merotz salchuyot).

## Who hears what

| Matter | Rabbinical Court | Family Court |
|--------|:---:|:---:|
| Grant the gett (Jewish couple) | ✔ exclusive | ✖ can't dissolve the religious marriage |
| Property division (izun mashabim) | ✔ if joined | ✔ |
| Child support (mezonot yeladim) | ✔ if joined | ✔ |
| Custody / parenting (best interests) | ✔ if joined | ✔ |
| Spousal support (mezonot isha) | ✔ | ✔ |
| Common-law partners (yeduim betzibur) | ✖ | ✔ |

## The race (merotz salchuyot)
- Each ancillary matter is captured by the court where it is *properly and completely* filed first.
- Because the two forums can apply different approaches, filing order is strategic.
- Validity/scope of an earlier filing is contestable — get a lawyer before conceding the forum.

## Other religions / civil-abroad marriages
- Muslim → Sharia courts; Druze → Druze courts; recognized Christian denominations → their ecclesiastical courts, for marriage/divorce.
- Interfaith couples, couples married civilly abroad, and those with no shared recognized religion generally use the Family Court and civil mechanisms.
- Do NOT apply the Jewish-couple / Rabbinical-Court template to these cases.
