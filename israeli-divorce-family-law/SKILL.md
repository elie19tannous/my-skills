# Israeli Divorce & Family Law

## Instructions

### Step 1: The Split-Jurisdiction Map
Israel has no civil marriage or civil divorce for many citizens; personal-status matters are split between religious and civil courts. Getting this map right is the single most important thing.

| Matter | Rabbinical Court (Beit Din Rabani) | Family Court (civil) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------|
| The divorce itself (Jewish couple) | Exclusive jurisdiction to grant/dissolve the marriage via the gett | No power to dissolve a Jewish religious marriage |
| Child support (mezonot yeladim) | Can hear if properly joined | Can hear |
| Custody / parenting (best interests) | Can hear if joined to the divorce | Can hear |
| Property division (izun mashabim) | Can hear if joined | Can hear |
| Spousal support (mezonot isha) | Typically | Can hear |

**The "race for jurisdiction" (merotz salchuyot):** for ancillary matters (property, support, custody) that both courts can hear, the court where the matter is *first properly filed* usually acquires jurisdiction over it. This creates a genuine strategic race at the start of a divorce. Filing choices here are consequential and are a core reason to involve a lawyer early.

**Other religions:** Muslim (Sharia courts), Druze, and recognized Christian denominations have their own religious courts for marriage/divorce. Interfaith or civil-abroad marriages, and couples with no shared recognized religion, are handled differently (often via the Family Court and civil mechanisms). This skill focuses on the common Jewish-couple scenario but flags when another regime applies.

### Step 2: The Gett (Jewish Religious Divorce)

| Concept | Hebrew | Details |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Gett | גט | The religious divorce writ; the marriage ends only when the husband gives, and the wife receives, the gett |
| Consent requirement | — | A gett must be given and received willingly; the Rabbinical Court can order but not physically force it |
| Gett refusal | סרבנות גט | When one spouse refuses to give/accept the gett to extort concessions |
| Chained spouse | עגונה / מסורבת גט | A spouse (classically the wife, agunah) unable to remarry because the gett is withheld |
| Sanctions on a refuser | — | The Rabbinical Court can impose pressures/sanctions under the Rabbinical Courts (Enforcement of Divorce Judgments) Law: restrictions on exit, licence, bank account, and even incarceration in extreme cases |

Gett refusal is a serious and gendered problem; when a user faces it, prioritize routing them to the Rabbinical Court's enforcement tools and a specialist lawyer/rabbinical advocate (to'enet rabanit).

### Step 3: Key Statutes

| Law | Hebrew | Year | Governs |
|-----|--------|------|---------|
| Spousal Property Relations Law | חוק יחסי ממון בין בני זוג | 1973 | Default property regime: resource balancing (izun mashabim) at divorce; heskem mamon (prenup) |
| Family Court Law | חוק בית המשפט לענייני משפחה | 1995 | Establishes and empowers the civil Family Court |
| Capacity and Guardianship Law | חוק הכשרות המשפטית והאפוטרופסות | 1962 | Parental responsibility, guardianship, best-interest standard |
| Rabbinical Courts (Enforcement of Divorce Judgments) Law | חוק בתי דין רבניים (קיום פסקי דין של גירושין) | 1995 | Sanctions against a gett refuser |
| Alimony (Assurance of Payment) Law | חוק המזונות (הבטחת תשלום) | 1972 | State advances of unpaid child support via NII |
| Amendment of Family Law (Maintenance) Law | חוק לתיקון דיני המשפחה (מזונות) | 1959 | Support obligations framework |

### Step 4: Property Division — Resource Balancing (Izun Mashabim)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Default regime | Under the 1973 law, assets accumulated during the marriage are balanced equally at divorce (izun mashabim), regardless of whose name they are in |
| What is balanced | Generally property acquired during the marriage through joint effort; pensions and savings accrued during the marriage are typically included |
| Usually excluded | Assets owned before the marriage, gifts, and inheritances received by one spouse — unless commingled or otherwise agreed |
| Timing | The balancing point is ordinarily the end of the marriage; courts can, in defined circumstances, order an earlier balancing |
| Prenup (heskem mamon) | Spouses can contract out of the default regime via a heskem mamon; to be valid it must be signed and approved/notarized as required (e.g. before a notary, court, or marriage registrar) |
| The family home | Often the central asset; specialized rules and protections (including for a resident spouse) can apply |

### Step 5: Child Support and Custody

| Concept | Hebrew | Details |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Child support | מזונות ילדים | Obligation to support minor children; historically fell more heavily on the father, but case law (notably the 2017 Supreme Court BAGATZ 919/15 line) shifted toward considering both parents' incomes and parenting time for ages 6-15 |
| Best interests of the child | טובת הילד | The governing standard for custody/parenting arrangements |
| Custody / parenting time | משמורת / זמני שהות | Modern practice favors detailed parenting plans and, often, shared/joint arrangements where appropriate |
| Support enforcement | הבטחת מזונות | If a parent doesn't pay, the National Insurance Institute (Bituach Leumi) can advance support and collect from the debtor, and the Execution Office can enforce |
| Guardianship | אפוטרופסות | Both parents are natural guardians; divorce does not by itself remove guardianship |

The old rule that fathers bear child support almost regardless of the mother's income is outdated for the 6-15 age band after the 2017 case law. Do not assert a flat "father always pays" rule.

### Step 6: The Divorce Sequence and Alternatives

| Step | Details |
|------|---------|
| 1. Get advice & choose forum | Because of the jurisdiction race, decide with a lawyer where to file ancillary matters first |
| 2. File | Open a divorce case in the Rabbinical Court (for the gett) and file ancillary matters in the chosen forum |
| 3. Mediation / MAHUT unit | Many family disputes route through the court's assistance/mediation unit (yehidat siyua) before litigation; a mandatory information-and-assessment meeting (MAHUT) applies to family disputes |
| 4. Interim orders | Temporary support, parenting, and asset-freezing orders can be sought early |
| 5. Resolve ancillary matters | Property balancing, support, parenting plan — by agreement or ruling |
| 6. Grant the gett | The Rabbinical Court arranges the gett ceremony; the marriage ends on completion |
| 7. Consent divorce (heskem gerushin) | A negotiated divorce agreement, once approved/given force of judgment, is faster and cheaper than a contested fight |

**Common-law partners (yeduim betzibur):** unmarried couples in a recognized shared-life relationship can acquire mutual rights (support, property, pension, tenancy succession) even without marriage; there is no "gett" to end it, but property and support disputes go to the Family Court.

## Examples

### Example 1: Where Do I File?
**Input:** "We're a Jewish couple divorcing. My spouse wants the Rabbinical Court, I've heard the Family Court is better for me. What matters?"
**Output:** The Rabbinical Court has exclusive power over the gett itself, so the divorce ends there regardless. The choice matters for the *ancillary* matters — property division, child and spousal support, custody — which both courts can hear. Because of the "race for jurisdiction" (merotz salchuyot), the court where each ancillary matter is properly filed first usually keeps it, and the two forums can apply different approaches. This is genuinely strategic and time-sensitive: consult a family lawyer immediately before filing anything, because filing order can lock in the forum.

### Example 2: Gett Refusal
**Input:** "My husband refuses to give me a gett unless I give up the apartment."
**Output:** This is gett refusal (sarvanut gett), and you may be a mesorevet gett. The Rabbinical Court can order the husband to give the gett and, under the Rabbinical Courts (Enforcement of Divorce Judgments) Law, impose escalating sanctions on a refuser — exit and licence restrictions, bank-account restrictions, and in extreme cases incarceration. Do not concede property to buy the gett without counsel; that is exactly the extortion the sanctions regime exists to counter. Engage a specialist divorce lawyer or rabbinical advocate (to'enet rabanit) and ask the court to invoke its enforcement powers.

### Example 3: Property Split
**Input:** "I bought our apartment before we married but we both lived in it for 15 years. Is it split 50/50?"
**Output:** Under the 1973 Spousal Property Relations Law, assets owned before the marriage are generally excluded from resource balancing (izun mashabim) — but 15 years of shared life, joint mortgage payments, renovations, or other commingling can create a claim to a share via "specific intent to share" (kavanat shituf sepcifit), especially for the family home. The outcome is fact-sensitive. Gather documentation of contributions and consult a lawyer; do not assume it is automatically either fully yours or a clean 50/50.

### Example 4: Child Support After 2017
**Input:** "We share the kids equally. Do I, the father, still pay full child support?"
**Output:** Not automatically. For children aged 6-15, the 2017 Supreme Court case law (BAGATZ 919/15) moved away from the old father-pays rule toward weighing both parents' incomes and the actual division of parenting time. With genuinely equal parenting time and comparable incomes, the support figure can be substantially reduced or balanced. The precise calculation is fact- and age-dependent (different rules apply under 6 and over 15). Present income and parenting-time evidence to the court and get a lawyer to model the figure.

## Gotchas

- The Rabbinical Court has EXCLUSIVE jurisdiction over the gett for a Jewish couple; the Family Court cannot dissolve the religious marriage. But ancillary matters (property, support, custody) can go to either court, and the filing race decides which. Agents that lump everything into "the divorce court" miss the whole strategic core.
- A gett cannot be physically forced; it must be given and received willingly. The Rabbinical Court's power is to order it and impose sanctions on a refuser, not to complete it against the parties' will. Do not tell a user the court can simply "issue" their divorce over a refuser.
- The old rule that fathers pay child support almost regardless of the mother's income is outdated for ages 6-15 after the 2017 BAGATZ 919/15 case law. Agents relying on training data may state a flat "father always pays" rule that is wrong.
- Pre-marital assets, gifts, and inheritances are generally excluded from resource balancing, but commingling or a proven intent to share (especially the family home) can pull them in. It is fact-sensitive, not a bright line.
- A prenup (heskem mamon) is only valid if executed and approved in the required form (before a notary, court, or marriage registrar). An informal signed paper may not bind. Agents sometimes treat any written agreement as an enforceable heskem mamon.
- Common-law partners (yeduim betzibur) can have real mutual rights (property, support, pension, tenancy succession) without marriage. Agents sometimes assume "not married = no claims," which is wrong.
- Family disputes typically route through a mandatory information/assessment meeting (MAHUT) and the court's assistance unit before full litigation. Skipping straight to "sue" misstates the process.
- Interfaith couples, couples married civilly abroad, and those with no shared recognized religion follow different rules; do not apply the Jewish-couple/Rabbinical-Court template to them.

## Troubleshooting

### Error: "My spouse filed in the other court first — am I stuck there?"
Cause: The race for jurisdiction (merotz salchuyot) means the first proper filing on an ancillary matter usually fixes the forum for that matter.
Solution: Do not assume it is settled. Whether the earlier filing was valid and complete, and which matters it actually captured, is contestable. Consult a family lawyer immediately to assess challenging jurisdiction or filing counterbalancing matters in the preferred forum before more issues are locked in.

### Error: "The Rabbinical Court ordered the gett but my spouse still refuses"
Cause: A gett cannot be forced; an order alone does not complete it.
Solution: Ask the Rabbinical Court to invoke its enforcement powers under the 1995 enforcement law — exit/licence/bank restrictions and, in extreme cases, incarceration of the refuser. Engage a specialist and document the refusal; these sanctions are the intended remedy for a sarvan/sarvanit gett.

### Error: "We agreed on everything — do we still need a court?"
Cause: Even a fully agreed divorce needs the gett and judicial approval to be binding.
Solution: Draft a divorce agreement (heskem gerushin), have it given force of judgment by the court, and complete the gett at the Rabbinical Court. A consent divorce is far faster and cheaper than a contested one, but the agreement is not self-executing without approval and the gett.
