# Scoping & Fee-Estimate Playbook

How to turn a captured matter into a written scope, a deadline register, and a defensible fee estimate.

## 1. Writing the Scope

Scope is a contract with the client about what the firm will and won't do. Three parts, all written:

**In scope** — stated as deliverables or phases, not vague activity.
- Good: "Draft and negotiate the SPA through to signing."
- Bad: "Assist with the transaction."

**Out of scope** — explicit exclusions. Unstated exclusions become client expectations.
- Examples: "does not include tax advice," "excludes enforcement of any judgment," "excludes appeals," "excludes competition/merger filings unless triggered."

**Assumptions** — the facts the scope and fee rely on. If one fails, scope and fee change.
- Examples: "assumes one target, one jurisdiction," "assumes two rounds of comments," "assumes documents provided promptly by client," "assumes matter uncontested."

**Client dependencies** — what the firm needs from the client and when (documents, approvals, decisions). Delay here shifts timelines and cost.

## 2. Deadline & Limitation Register

| Deadline | Type | Source (rule/clause) | Trigger date | Drop-dead date | Reminder lead | Risk |
|----------|------|----------------------|--------------|----------------|---------------|------|
| e.g. claim time-bar | Limitation | statute § / prescription rule | date cause accrued | computed | 6 months | HIGH |
| e.g. notice period | Contractual | clause X | event date | computed | 2 weeks | Med |

Rules:
- The **limitation/prescription** date is the highest-stakes row. If unknown or contested → HIGH, resolve first.
- Always record the **source and the calculation**, never a bare date. A date with no rule/trigger is unverifiable.
- Every row gets a **diarised lead-time reminder** before the drop-dead date.

## 3. Fee Build-Up

Never pluck a number. Build it from the scope.

**Basis** — pick and state one: fixed fee / hourly / capped / phased / contingency (subject to local professional rules; contingency is restricted or prohibited in some jurisdictions/matter types).

**Build-up:**
- Hourly/phased: estimated hours × rate, broken out by phase and fee-earner grade.
- Fixed: the rationale for the fixed number (comparable matters, scoped effort).

**Disbursements** — listed separately, never buried in the fee: court/filing fees, experts, counsel, agents, translation, travel, search fees.

**Tax** — VAT/sales tax shown as a separate line, not silently baked in.

**Range & triggers** — where uncertain, give a range and name what pushes it up:
- opponent contests / defends
- extra parties join
- additional rounds of negotiation or amendment
- new issues discovered in diligence
- expedition / urgent timetable

## 4. Estimate vs Quote

State which it is — the difference is legal and commercial:
- **Estimate:** a good-faith projection; can move if scope/assumptions change. Say so and give a range.
- **Quote / fixed fee:** a commitment for the defined scope. Only give one when scope is tight enough to defend it. An under-scoped fixed fee is a loss or a dispute.

## 5. Always Attach Scope to the Number

A fee figure travels with its scope-in, scope-out, and assumptions — always. A number alone is a future dispute. The client should never be able to say "I thought that covered X" when X was expressly out of scope.

## 6. Recommendation Framing

Close with one of:
- **Open** — gates cleared, proceed.
- **Decline** — conflict, no viable fee, or outside firm's practice.
- **Hold / take-subject-to** — proceed only once a named condition is met (conflict waiver signed, limitation confirmed, retainer received, AML/KYC completed).
