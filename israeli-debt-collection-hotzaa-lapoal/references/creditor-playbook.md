# Creditor Playbook — Opening & Enforcing an Execution File

## 1. Pick the right track

| You hold | Track |
|----------|-------|
| A court money judgment (psak din) | Judgment enforcement |
| A check, promissory note, or bill (shtar) | Execution of a shtar — no prior lawsuit |
| A written, liquidated debt up to the statutory ceiling | Fixed-sum claim (tviah al sechum katzuv) |
| A mortgage / registered pledge | Realization of collateral |

## 2. Documents to attach

- The underlying instrument (judgment / original shtar / signed debt document)
- For a bounced check: the bank's return notice
- Debtor identifying details (name, ID/company number, last known address)
- Calculation of the balance (principal + interest + linkage to filing date)

## 3. Sequence after filing

1. Pay the opening fee (added to the debt, recoverable from the debtor).
2. Debtor is served a warning (azhara) with a period (~20-30 days) to pay or act.
3. After the warning period with no payment/opposition, request measures.

## 4. Enforcement measures (request in order of proportionality)

1. Third-party garnishment (ikul tzad gimel) — bank accounts, employer/wages, tenants.
2. Asset attachment — movable property; lien on real estate; vehicle lien/clamp.
3. Ability investigation — compel an asset declaration (tatzhir) under oath.
4. Restrictions (hagbalot) — exit ban, driving-licence restriction, restricted bank customer, company-management block.
5. Order to appear (tzav havaa) — narrow, for an evading debtor under a payment order.

## 5. Watch the balance

The file accrues CPI linkage + interest from the instrument date, plus fees per action. Request an updated yitrat chov before settlement talks.

## Pitfalls
- Ensure valid service; defective service unwinds enforcement.
- Respect the debtor's protected-income floor on wage garnishments — over-garnishing invites a correction motion and delay.
- Do not use this track for tax debts (ITA/VAT/NII collect separately).
