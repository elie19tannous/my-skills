# Israeli Consumer Protection

## Instructions

### Step 1: Legal Framework
Israeli consumer rights are anchored in a handful of statutes and regulations:

| Law | Hebrew | Year | Key Provisions |
|-----|--------|------|----------------|
| Consumer Protection Law | חוק הגנת הצרכן | 5741-1981 | Core statute: cancellation rights, disclosure duties, ban on deception |
| Consumer Protection Regulations (Cancellation of Transaction) | תקנות הגנת הצרכן (ביטול עסקה) | 5771-2010 | The "bitul iska" regulations: 14-day return right for many goods/services |
| Sale Law | חוק המכר | 1968 | Implied conformity terms, remedy for non-conforming goods |
| Standards Law | חוק התקנים | 1953 | Product safety and mandatory standards |
| Warranty Regulations (electrical goods) | תקנות אחריות (מוצרי חשמל) | — | Minimum warranty periods for appliances |

**Two distinct cancellation regimes** are frequently confused:
- **Cancellation of a "distance" or "door-to-door" transaction** (section 14ג / 14ג1): a strong statutory cooling-off right for transactions made by phone, internet, mail, or outside the seller's business premises.
- **General "bitul iska" (cancellation of transaction) regulations, 2010**: a broader right to change your mind and return many goods/services within 14 days regardless of how the deal was made, subject to the goods being unused and a cancellation fee.

Do NOT conflate the two — they have different windows, conditions, and fee rules.

### Step 2: Distance & Door-to-Door Sales (section 14ג)
A transaction made remotely (phone, internet, catalog, mail) or off-premises (door-to-door, iska rochelet) carries a heightened cancellation right:

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Cancellation window (goods) | From the transaction date until 14 days after receiving the goods |
| Cancellation window (services) | From the transaction date until 14 days after the contract or the written disclosure document, whichever is later |
| Extended window (accessibility) | For a consumer who is a person with disability, a senior citizen (65+), or a new immigrant, the window is 4 months, provided the transaction included a conversation (including electronic) |
| Refund | Seller refunds within 14 days of the cancellation notice |
| Cancellation fee | The lower of 5% of the transaction price or 100 NIS, and only where the cancellation is a change of mind (not a defect) |
| Return shipping | Consumer may bear return cost unless the item is defective or the seller misrepresented it |
| Defect / mismatch | If the item is defective or does not match its description, no cancellation fee and the seller bears return costs |

### Step 3: General Cancellation Right ("Bitul Iska" Regulations 2010)
Even for in-store purchases, the 2010 regulations give a broad right to cancel a wide range of goods and services:

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Window (most goods) | 14 days from purchase (or from receiving the goods, if later) |
| Condition | Goods must be unused, undamaged, with no deterioration, generally with original packaging where relevant |
| Cancellation fee | The lower of 5% of the price or 100 NIS |
| Refund method | Same method used to pay; refund within 14 days of the cancellation notice |
| Services | Cancellable within 14 days of the transaction, provided performance has not yet begun (for time-bound services, different rules apply) |

**Common exclusions from the general 14-day return right** (verify current list — it changes):

| Excluded category | Note |
|-------------------|------|
| Perishable goods (food) | מוצרים מתכלים |
| Custom / made-to-order goods | טובין שיוצרו במיוחד לצרכן |
| Goods that can be copied/recorded | after opening original packaging (software, media) |
| Intimate/hygiene products | e.g. underwear, if opened |
| Lodging, travel, and event tickets | separate cancellation rules under the distance-sales regime |
| Medicines and dietary supplements | — |

### Step 4: Warranties and Non-Conforming Goods
Distinguish contractual warranty from statutory conformity rights:

| Concept | Hebrew | Details |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Manufacturer/seller warranty | אחריות | Voluntary or regulation-mandated coverage for defects for a stated period |
| Electrical-goods minimum warranty | אחריות מוצרי חשמל | Regulations set minimum warranty periods (commonly 1 year, longer for some appliances) and require local service |
| Statutory conformity (Sale Law) | התאמה | Goods must match description, sample, and fitness for ordinary purpose; a non-conforming good can be rejected or repaired/replaced |
| Repair-time obligation | חובת תיקון | Warranty repairs must be completed within a defined timeframe; repeated failed repairs may entitle the consumer to a replacement or refund |

### Step 5: Deception, Misleading Advertising, and Overcharging
The law prohibits misleading the consumer:

| Prohibition | Hebrew | Details |
|-------------|--------|---------|
| Deception (section 2) | הטעיה | Misleading a consumer on any material matter (quality, quantity, price, ingredients, prior use) is prohibited |
| Undue influence / exploitation (section 3) | ניצול מצוקה | Exploiting a consumer's distress, disability, or ignorance to conclude an unreasonable transaction |
| Price display duty | חובת הצגת מחיר | Prices must be clearly displayed; the displayed/checkout price binds the seller |
| Misleading advertising | פרסום מטעה | False or misleading ads are prohibited and can trigger authority sanctions |

### Step 6: Filing a Complaint and Enforcement
When self-resolution fails, escalate:

| Channel | Hebrew | Best For | Cost |
|---------|--------|----------|------|
| Direct written demand to seller | מכתב דרישה | First step, most disputes | Free |
| Consumer Protection & Fair Trade Authority | הרשות להגנת הצרכן ולסחר הוגן | Deception, refusal to honor cancellation, misleading ads; regulatory sanctions | Free |
| Consumer Council | המועצה לצרכנות | Guidance, mediation, class-complaint intake | Free / low |
| Small Claims Court | תביעות קטנות | Money claims up to the current cap | Filing fee (~1% of claim) |
| Class action | תובענה ייצוגית | Systemic overcharging affecting many consumers | Via counsel |

**Escalation sequence:** (1) written cancellation/demand to the seller with a deadline; (2) if refused, file with the Authority and/or the Consumer Council; (3) if money is owed, file in small claims court. Keep every receipt, order confirmation, ad screenshot, and message thread.

## Examples

### Example 1: Cancelling an Online Purchase
**Input:** "I bought a jacket online 5 days ago, it arrived yesterday and I want to return it."
**Output:** This is a distance transaction (iska bemachar rachok). Under section 14ג the consumer may cancel from the order date until 14 days after receiving the goods. Send a written cancellation notice to the seller. Because it is a change of mind (not a defect), the seller may charge a cancellation fee of the lower of 5% of the price or 100 NIS, and the consumer may bear return shipping. The seller must refund the balance within 14 days of the cancellation notice, using the original payment method. Keep the order confirmation and the cancellation notice.

### Example 2: Defective Appliance Under Warranty
**Input:** "My fridge broke after 8 months, the store says the warranty doesn't cover it."
**Output:** Electrical-goods warranty regulations set a minimum warranty period (commonly at least 1 year for many appliances). An 8-month-old fridge is very likely within the mandatory warranty. The seller/importer must repair the defect within the regulated timeframe. Send a written repair demand citing the warranty regulations. If repairs repeatedly fail, the consumer may be entitled to a replacement or refund. If the seller refuses, file a complaint with the Consumer Protection & Fair Trade Authority and, for the money, small claims court.

### Example 3: Misleading Price
**Input:** "The shelf said 49 NIS but the register charged 79 NIS."
**Output:** The displayed price is binding. Charging more than the displayed price is a price-display violation and can be deception under section 2. Ask the store to honor the shelf price or refund the difference on the spot; photograph the shelf label. If refused, file a complaint with the Consumer Protection & Fair Trade Authority (which can sanction the retailer) and, for the overcharge, claim in small claims court.

### Example 4: Door-to-Door Sale to a Senior
**Input:** "A salesperson signed my 70-year-old father up for an expensive vacuum at his door. Can he cancel?"
**Output:** This is an off-premises transaction (iska rochelet). The standard cooling-off window is 14 days, but because the consumer is a senior citizen (65+) and the transaction involved a conversation, the extended cancellation window of 4 months applies. Send a written cancellation notice within that window; the seller must refund within 14 days and arrange collection of the goods. If the sale exploited the consumer's situation, section 3 (undue influence) may also apply. Escalate to the Authority if the seller resists.

## Gotchas

- The two cancellation regimes are distinct. The distance/off-premises right (section 14ג) and the general 2010 bitul-iska regulations have different triggers, windows, and fee rules. Agents frequently merge them into one "14-day return" rule and get the conditions wrong.
- The cancellation fee is capped at the LOWER of 5% of the price or 100 NIS, and only applies to a change-of-mind cancellation. For a defective or misdescribed item there is no cancellation fee and the seller bears return costs. Agents sometimes apply the fee even to defect returns.
- The extended 4-month cancellation window applies to persons with disability, senior citizens (65+), and new immigrants, but only where the transaction included a conversation. Agents either forget this protection or apply it to every consumer.
- Not everything is returnable. The 2010 regulations exclude perishables, custom-made goods, opened copyable media, intimate/hygiene items, and certain services. The exclusion list is amended periodically — verify it rather than asserting a blanket 14-day return.
- "Warranty" (achrayut, a contractual/regulatory promise) is not the same as statutory conformity under the Sale Law. A consumer may have a remedy for a non-conforming good even after a voluntary warranty expires. Agents sometimes treat an expired warranty as the end of all rights.
- The displayed price binds the seller. Charging above the shelf/checkout price is a violation, not a "system error" the consumer must absorb.
- The small claims court jurisdiction cap changes periodically. Verify the current threshold before advising a consumer to sue.
- Refund timelines run from the cancellation notice, not from when the seller "gets around to it." The seller must refund within 14 days.

## Troubleshooting

### Error: "Seller refuses to accept a return within the 14-day window"
Cause: Some sellers claim a "no returns" policy or that the item is final sale.
Solution: A store policy cannot override the statutory cancellation right. Send a written cancellation notice citing the applicable regime (section 14ג for distance/off-premises, or the 2010 bitul-iska regulations for a general return) and demand a refund within 14 days minus at most the capped cancellation fee. If the seller still refuses, file with the Consumer Protection & Fair Trade Authority and, for the money, small claims court.

### Error: "Seller withheld the full refund as a 'restocking fee'"
Cause: Some sellers charge inflated restocking or handling fees.
Solution: The lawful cancellation fee for a change-of-mind cancellation is capped at the lower of 5% of the price or 100 NIS. Any charge above that is unlawful. For a defective item there is no fee at all. Demand the difference in writing; escalate to the Authority if refused.

### Error: "The advertised price differed from the checkout price"
Cause: Bait pricing or a genuine labeling error.
Solution: The displayed price binds the seller. Ask for the item at the displayed price or a refund of the difference, and photograph the label. If the seller refuses, this is a price-display and potential deception violation reportable to the Authority, with the overcharge recoverable in small claims court.
